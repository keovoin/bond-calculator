Understanding Bonds

A beginner-friendly guide to bond investment.

What is a Bond?

A bond is a fixed-income instrument that represents a loan made by an investor to a borrower (typically a corporation or government). Think of it as an "IOU" with a defined repayment schedule and interest rate.

Fixed Income Coupon Maturity Principal Issuer Face Value Yield Tenor Amortization WHT

Key Terms Explained

Coupon Rate
The annual interest rate paid by the issuer. For example, 8.5% p.a. means you earn 8.5% of your principal each year, paid monthly.
Tenor / Maturity
The length of time until the bond expires and your principal is fully returned. A 5-year bond matures 5 years from the issue date.
Principal (Face Value)
The original amount you invest. This is repaid over time through the amortization schedule.
Amortization
How the principal is repaid. In a straight-line amortization, equal portions of the principal are returned each year on the anniversary date.
Withholding Tax (WHT)
A tax deducted from your coupon interest before it reaches your account. Residents pay 6%, non-residents pay 14%.
Nominal / Unit Price
The price per single bond unit (e.g. USD 25). Your investment amount must be a multiple of this value.

Benefits of Bond Investment

✅
Predictable Income

Bonds pay a fixed coupon on a known schedule. You know exactly how much you'll earn each month — no surprises.

🔒
Lower Risk than Stocks

Bond holders are creditors, not owners. In a liquidation, bondholders are paid before shareholders. Principal repayment is contractually guaranteed.

📈
Capital Preservation

Unlike stocks whose value fluctuates daily, bonds return your full principal over the tenor via amortization.

🏦
Portfolio Diversification

Bonds have low correlation with equity markets. Adding bonds to a portfolio reduces overall volatility.

💰
Regular Cash Flow

Monthly coupon payments provide steady cash flow — ideal for institutions and investors who need liquidity.

Bonds vs. Stocks — Comparison

Feature Bonds Stocks
Return Type Fixed coupon (predictable) Dividends + capital gains (variable)
Risk Level Lower — contractual obligation to pay Higher — market-dependent
Income Schedule Monthly / quarterly (guaranteed dates) Quarterly dividends (not guaranteed)
Principal Return Repaid in full over the tenor No guarantee — depends on market price
Priority in Liquidation Paid before shareholders Last to be paid (residual claim)
Ownership You are a creditor (lender) You are an owner (equity holder)
Market Volatility Low — price is relatively stable High — prices move daily
Suitable For Conservative investors, institutions, steady income Growth-oriented investors, higher risk tolerance
Summary: Bonds are ideal for investors who value certainty and steady income over high-risk, high-reward strategies. They are often used as the stable foundation of a diversified portfolio.

Coupons are accrued daily and credited on the scheduled date with no holiday delay. Default interest rate (coupon + spread) applies only to late coupon payments and is calculated manually case by case.
